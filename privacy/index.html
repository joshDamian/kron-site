Kron privacy policy

Effective 14 October 2026 · Kron is made by Damian Labs

Kron is a personal log. You write short entries about your days, and Lumen, Kron's built-in intelligence, reads them back to you. This policy explains what happens to what you write.

The short version: Damian Labs never receives your entries. Kron has no accounts and no servers of its own. Everything you write is stored on your phone.

What Kron stores, and where

  • Your entries, closed days, tags, patterns, profile, practices, dashboards, chats and settings are stored in a database on your phone.
  • Your AI key is stored in your phone's secure storage (Android Keystore). It is never put in backups and never sent anywhere except to the AI provider it belongs to.
  • A short problem log (up to 200 entries, kept 30 days) records technical problems so you can see them in Settings. It never contains your entries, your prompts or your key, and it stays on your phone.

What leaves your phone

Lumen works by sending text to an AI provider that you choose and connect with your own key: Anthropic, OpenAI, or another OpenAI-compatible service. Kron sends it directly from your phone to that provider over an encrypted connection. Damian Labs is not in the middle and cannot see it. Kron sends:

  • After you save an entry: its text, so Lumen can tag it.
  • When you close a day, or ask Lumen to reread one: that day's entries.
  • When you chat: your question and the entries and day summaries it needs, which can be every non-private entry in the period you're asking about.
  • When you build or refresh a dashboard: your non-private entries on closed days.
  • For patterns, your profile, practices and the early read: summaries of your closed days.
  • With any of these: what you told Kron you want to understand, and the notes you've given Lumen.

Entries you mark private are never sent to any AI provider and are left out of Share with AI. If you haven't connected a key, nothing is sent.

Your AI provider handles that text under its own terms and privacy policy, which you agreed to when you created your key. Please read them: for example Anthropic and OpenAI.

Things you choose to share

  • Backups are copies of your database saved to a folder you pick, such as one in Google Drive, or shared through Android's share sheet.
  • Share with AI builds text from a day, tag or range and hands it to the app you pick. You see it before it goes.
  • Reporting a Lumen reply. If you long-press something Lumen wrote and choose Report, Kron sends Damian Labs the reason you picked, your optional note, the text of that one reply, the part of the app it came from and Kron's version. It goes through Google Forms. Your entries are never included, though a reply can quote them. We use reports only to make Lumen safer, keep them no longer than 12 months, and delete one on request.
  • Android backup. If Android's own device backup is on, Android may include Kron's data in your Google account backup, as it does for other apps. Your AI key is excluded.

What Kron doesn't do

No accounts. No advertising. No analytics or tracking. No selling of your data. No location, contacts, photos or microphone.

Permissions

  • Notifications: your reminders, and a note when a long Lumen read is ready.
  • Foreground service (data sync): lets a long Lumen read, such as closing a day, finish if you switch apps. A notification shows while it runs.
  • Biometrics: only if you turn on app lock. Kron never sees your fingerprint or face. Android checks them.
  • Internet: to reach your AI provider, and to send a report when you choose to.

Deleting your data

You can delete any entry in the app. Uninstalling Kron, or clearing its storage in Android settings, deletes everything Kron stored on your phone. Remove your key in Settings → Lumen. Text already sent to your AI provider is governed by that provider's retention policy. Backups you saved are yours to delete.

Children

Kron is meant for adults (18+) and is not directed at children.

Changes

If this policy changes, we'll update this page and its date. Material changes will also be noted in the app's release notes.
